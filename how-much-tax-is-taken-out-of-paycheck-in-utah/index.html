How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in Utah?

On a typical Utah salary of $93,400, a single filer has about 24.6% withheld: $23,019 a year, or $1,918 a month. That ranks Utah 41 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where Utah sits

Utah ranks 41 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: Delaware at 24.1%, Minnesota at 24.3%, Connecticut at 24.7%, Virginia at 24.7%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. Utah sits 8.1% above the lightest and 2.6% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $93,400, is Utah's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in Utah.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
