How Nevada taxes wages

Nevada does not levy a tax on wage income, so your state withholding is zero. Federal income tax and FICA still apply — they are federal, not state, and every state pays them. That is why a no-income-tax state still withholds roughly 18.1% of a $76,400 salary.

Nevada is one of 9 jurisdictions with no wage income tax. The $13,823 withheld here is entirely federal: $7,978 income tax and $5,845 FICA.

Federal tax applies everywhere

Whatever the state does, the federal side is identical. On $76,400 a single filer pays $7,978 of federal income tax after the $16,100 standard deduction, plus $5,845 in Social Security and Medicare.

What is different about Nevada

  • No wage income tax applies to this salary.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $76,400 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $76,400 lands in Nevada

Nevada households have a median income of $76,364, ranking 23 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $76,400 is 100% of the Nevada median and 98% of the national one — $36 above it.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 40th-60th percentile of Nevada households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
