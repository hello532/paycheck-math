The tax structure you hit at $50/hour in Georgia

Your first $16,100 of gross pay is covered by the federal standard deduction, so federal income tax starts above that line. Georgia adds its own $12,000 deduction before state tax starts.

What five hours of overtime actually adds

At time-and-a-half, five extra hours a week is $19,500 more gross a year. After federal tax, Georgia tax and FICA, that lands as $12,672 — about $48.74 for each of those extra hours.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.

What is different about Georgia

  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $104,000 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $104,000 lands in Georgia

Georgia households have a median income of $74,632, ranking 26 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $104,000 is 139% of the Georgia median and 134% of the national one — $29,368 above it.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 60th-80th percentile of Georgia households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.
