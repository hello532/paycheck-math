How Alabama taxes wages

Alabama uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $62,200 salary the state tax comes to $2,920, an effective 4.7% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

Alabama taxes its first dollar at 2.0%; the band containing $62,200 starts at $0 and charges 5.0%. Going from $62,200 to $77,750 adds $778 of state tax.

What is specific to Alabama here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 24.7% in Alabama, against 19.7% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about Alabama

  • Local income taxes add about 0.07% of AGI on average in Alabama, on top of the state rate. County and city taxes are excluded from the calculator above.
  • Federal income tax is deductible on the state return. Alabama lets filers subtract some or all federal tax paid from state taxable income, which lowers the real state bill below what the flat table shows.
  • 3 income bands. The top band starts at $3,000 of taxable income and charges 5.0%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $62,200 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $62,200 lands in Alabama

Alabama households have a median income of $62,212, ranking 45 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $62,200 is 100% of the Alabama median and 80% of the national one — $12 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of Alabama households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
