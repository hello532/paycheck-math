How Montana taxes wages

Montana uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $70,800 salary the state tax comes to $2,639, an effective 3.7% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

Montana taxes its first dollar at 4.7%; the band containing $70,800 starts at $0 and charges 5.7%. Going from $70,800 to $88,500 adds $1,000 of state tax.

What is specific to Montana here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 35.3% in Montana, against 29.6% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about Montana

  • Federal income tax is deductible on the state return. Montana lets filers subtract some or all federal tax paid from state taxable income, which lowers the real state bill below what the flat table shows.
  • The state income starting point includes the federal standard deduction. Montana builds its state taxable income on top of the federal figure, so the $16,100 federal deduction is already reflected.
  • 2 income bands. The top band starts at $47,500 of taxable income and charges 5.7%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $70,800 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $70,800 lands in Montana

Montana households have a median income of $70,804, ranking 35 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $70,800 is 100% of the Montana median and 91% of the national one — $4 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of Montana households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
