How South Carolina taxes wages

South Carolina uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $67,800 salary the state tax comes to $2,911, an effective 4.3% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

South Carolina taxes its first dollar at 0.0%; the band containing $67,800 starts at $0 and charges 6.0%. Going from $67,800 to $84,750 adds $1,017 of state tax.

What is specific to South Carolina here

What is different about South Carolina

  • The state income starting point includes the federal standard deduction. South Carolina builds its state taxable income on top of the federal figure, so the $16,100 federal deduction is already reflected.
  • 3 income bands. The top band starts at $18,230 of taxable income and charges 6.0%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $67,800 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $67,800 lands in South Carolina

South Carolina households have a median income of $67,804, ranking 41 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $67,800 is 100% of the South Carolina median and 87% of the national one — $4 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of South Carolina households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
