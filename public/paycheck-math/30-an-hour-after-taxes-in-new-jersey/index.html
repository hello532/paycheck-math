The tax structure you hit at $30/hour in New Jersey

Your first $16,100 of gross pay is covered by the federal standard deduction, so federal income tax starts above that line. New Jersey has no standard deduction for this calculation, so state tax applies from the first dollar.

What five hours of overtime actually adds

At time-and-a-half, five extra hours a week is $11,700 more gross a year. After federal tax, New Jersey tax and FICA, that lands as $7,994 — about $30.75 for each of those extra hours.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.

What is different about New Jersey

  • Some cities and counties levy their own payroll or wage taxes in New Jersey. Those are local, not state, and excluded here.
  • 7 income bands. The top band starts at $1,000,000 of taxable income and charges 10.8%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $62,400 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $62,400 lands in New Jersey

New Jersey households have a median income of $99,781, ranking 3 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $62,400 is 63% of the New Jersey median and 80% of the national one — $37,381 below the state midpoint.
