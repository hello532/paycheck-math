How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in Idaho?

On a typical Idaho salary of $74,900, a single filer has about 22.0% withheld: $16,494 a year, or $1,375 a month. That ranks Idaho 31 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where Idaho sits

Idaho ranks 31 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: Nebraska at 21.4%, Georgia at 21.8%, Kansas at 22.3%, Rhode Island at 22.5%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. Idaho sits 5.4% above the lightest and 5.2% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $74,900, is Idaho's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in Idaho.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
