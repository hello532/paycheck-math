How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in New Hampshire?

On a typical New Hampshire salary of $96,800, a single filer has about 20.5% withheld: $19,871 a year, or $1,656 a month. That ranks New Hampshire 23 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where New Hampshire sits

New Hampshire ranks 23 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: North Carolina at 20.4%, Arizona at 20.5%, Pennsylvania at 20.8%, Montana at 20.9%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. New Hampshire sits 3.9% above the lightest and 6.7% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $96,800, is New Hampshire's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in New Hampshire.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
