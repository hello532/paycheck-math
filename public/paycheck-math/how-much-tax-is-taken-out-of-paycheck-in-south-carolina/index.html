How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in South Carolina?

On a typical South Carolina salary of $67,800, a single filer has about 20.9% withheld: $14,184 a year, or $1,182 a month. That ranks South Carolina 26 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where South Carolina sits

South Carolina ranks 26 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: Pennsylvania at 20.8%, Montana at 20.9%, Michigan at 21.1%, Wisconsin at 21.3%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. South Carolina sits 4.3% above the lightest and 6.3% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $67,800, is South Carolina's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in South Carolina.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
