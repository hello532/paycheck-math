How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in Vermont?

On a typical Vermont salary of $81,200, a single filer has about 22.8% withheld: $18,495 a year, or $1,541 a month. That ranks Vermont 34 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where Vermont sits

Vermont ranks 34 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: Kansas at 22.3%, Rhode Island at 22.5%, Maine at 23.3%, New York at 23.6%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. Vermont sits 6.2% above the lightest and 4.5% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $81,200, is Vermont's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in Vermont.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
