How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in Virginia?

On a typical Virginia salary of $89,900, a single filer has about 24.7% withheld: $22,234 a year, or $1,853 a month. That ranks Virginia 43 of 50 for paycheck withholding among the states here.

Where Virginia sits

Virginia ranks 43 of 50 on paycheck withholding. The states immediately around it: Utah at 24.6%, Connecticut at 24.7%, New Jersey at 25.0%, Maryland at 25.2%.

The lightest state measured here is Tennessee at 16.6%; the heaviest is Washington DC at 27.2%. Virginia sits 8.1% above the lightest and 2.5% below the heaviest.

The salary used here, $89,900, is Virginia's median household income from 2023 — so this is what a typical local earner sees, not what a national average salary would produce in Virginia.

What actually changes your number

Three things move withholding more than the state does: filing status, pre-tax deductions and additional income. A 401(k) contribution comes out before federal income tax, so putting in 5% lowers the federal line without lowering the FICA line. Filing jointly widens the federal brackets, which is why two similar salaries can withhold very differently.
