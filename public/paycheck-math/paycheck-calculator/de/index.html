How Delaware taxes wages

Delaware uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $82,200 salary the state tax comes to $4,238, an effective 5.2% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

Delaware taxes its first dollar at 2.2%; the band containing $82,200 starts at $0 and charges 6.6%. Going from $82,200 to $102,750 adds $1,356 of state tax.

What is specific to Delaware here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 36.3% in Delaware, against 29.6% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about Delaware

  • Some cities and counties levy their own payroll or wage taxes in Delaware. Those are local, not state, and excluded here.
  • 6 income bands. The top band starts at $60,000 of taxable income and charges 6.6%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $82,200 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $82,200 lands in Delaware

Delaware households have a median income of $82,174, ranking 16 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $82,200 is 100% of the Delaware median and 106% of the national one — $26 above it.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 40th-60th percentile of Delaware households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

Where Delaware income sits in the tax structure

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
