How Florida taxes wages

Florida does not levy a tax on wage income, so your state withholding is zero. Federal income tax and FICA still apply — they are federal, not state, and every state pays them. That is why a no-income-tax state still withholds roughly 17.6% of a $73,300 salary.

Florida is one of 9 jurisdictions with no wage income tax. The $12,903 withheld here is entirely federal: $7,296 income tax and $5,607 FICA.

Federal tax applies everywhere

Whatever the state does, the federal side is identical. On $73,300 a single filer pays $7,296 of federal income tax after the $16,100 standard deduction, plus $5,607 in Social Security and Medicare.

What is different about Florida

  • No wage income tax applies to this salary.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $73,300 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $73,300 lands in Florida

Florida households have a median income of $73,311, ranking 31 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $73,300 is 100% of the Florida median and 94% of the national one — $11 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of Florida households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
