How Georgia taxes wages

Georgia applies a flat 5.0% rate to taxable wages after a $15,000 standard deduction. A flat rate means the percentage does not rise with income, but the dollar amount does: at $50,000 you pay about $1,747, at $100,000 about $4,242.

Georgia is one of 14 states with a single wage-tax rate. Its first $15,000 of gross wages is untaxed, which means the $74,600 salary pays state tax on $59,600.

Federal tax applies everywhere

Whatever the state does, the federal side is identical. On $74,600 a single filer pays $7,582 of federal income tax after the $16,100 standard deduction, plus $5,707 in Social Security and Medicare.

What is specific to Georgia here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 34.6% in Georgia, against 29.6% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about Georgia

  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $74,600 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $74,600 lands in Georgia

Georgia households have a median income of $74,632, ranking 26 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $74,600 is 100% of the Georgia median and 96% of the national one — $32 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of Georgia households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

Where Georgia income sits in the tax structure

Your first $16,100 of gross pay is covered by the federal standard deduction, so federal income tax starts above that line. Georgia adds its own $15,000 deduction before state tax starts.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
