How Maryland taxes wages

Maryland uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $98,700 salary the state tax comes to $4,477, an effective 4.5% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

Maryland taxes its first dollar at 2.0%; the band containing $98,700 starts at $0 and charges 4.8%. Going from $98,700 to $123,375 adds $1,222 of state tax.

What is specific to Maryland here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 34.4% in Maryland, against 29.6% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about Maryland

  • Local income taxes add about 2.4% of AGI on average in Maryland, on top of the state rate. County and city taxes are excluded from the calculator above.
  • 10 income bands. The top band starts at $1,000,000 of taxable income and charges 6.5%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $98,700 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $98,700 lands in Maryland

Maryland households have a median income of $98,678, ranking 4 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $98,700 is 100% of the Maryland median and 127% of the national one — $22 above it.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 40th-60th percentile of Maryland households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

Where Maryland income sits in the tax structure

Your first $16,100 of gross pay is covered by the federal standard deduction, so federal income tax starts above that line. Maryland adds its own $3,350 deduction before state tax starts.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
