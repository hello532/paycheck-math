How West Virginia taxes wages

West Virginia uses progressive brackets, so only the income inside each band is taxed at that band's rate. On a $55,900 salary the state tax comes to $1,871, an effective 3.3% — lower than the top bracket you reach.

West Virginia taxes its first dollar at 2.2%; the band containing $55,900 starts at $0 and charges 4.4%. Going from $55,900 to $69,875 adds $658 of state tax.

What is specific to West Virginia here

Your marginal rate — what you lose on the next dollar earned — is 24.1% in West Virginia, against 19.7% in Texas. That is the number that matters for overtime, a raise or a second job.

What is different about West Virginia

  • Some cities and counties levy their own payroll or wage taxes in West Virginia. Those are local, not state, and excluded here.
  • 5 income bands. The top band starts at $60,000 of taxable income and charges 4.8%.
  • Social Security stops at $184,500 of wages. At $55,900 you are below that line, so the full 6.2% applies to every paycheck.

Where $55,900 lands in West Virginia

West Virginia households have a median income of $55,948, ranking 50 of 51 jurisdictions against a national median of $77,719. A gross salary of $55,900 is 100% of the West Virginia median and 72% of the national one — $48 below the state midpoint.

By percentile, that puts a single earner in the 20th-40th percentile of West Virginia households. Read this as a rough marker, not an exact position: the percentiles count whole households, which often have two earners, while this salary is one person's gross.

Where West Virginia income sits in the tax structure

Your first $16,100 of gross pay is covered by the federal standard deduction, so federal income tax starts above that line. West Virginia has no standard deduction for this calculation, so state tax applies from the first dollar.

What this page does not model

Single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k), no health premium, no dependents, no local or city income tax, and state credits and personal exemptions are excluded — which means the state figure is a slight overstatement. Self-employment would double the FICA share.
