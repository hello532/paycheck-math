What Percentage of a Kansas Paycheck Goes to Taxes?

At a typical Kansas salary of $70,300, a single filer loses 22.3% to taxes and keeps $54,652. The percentage is not one number — it is three, and they behave differently.

Two of those lines are flat nationwide: Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. The federal line is progressive, so its effective rate (9.4%) is well below the bracket you are in. The state line is what makes Kansas different from its neighbours.

What $70,300 means in Kansas

That gross is the Kansas median household income ($70,333, 2023), rounded to the nearest hundred. It is 90% of the national median household income of $77,719.

After every deduction you keep $54,652 a year — $4,554 a month, $2,102 per biweekly check. Rent at the common 30%-of-gross rule would take $1,758 a month, leaving $2,797.

At this level Kansas takes a little under a quarter of gross pay. A $5,000 raise would add about $3,239 to take-home, because the marginal rate applies only to the extra.

How to lower the percentage

Pre-tax deductions come off before federal income tax: a 401(k), a health premium or an HSA. None of them reduce FICA, so the saving is real but partial. Putting 5% of $70,300 into a 401(k) removes $3,515 from the federal taxable base, roughly $773 of federal tax at this income. Filing jointly, a dependent-care account and the earned income credit change the result too.
