What Percentage of a Massachusetts Paycheck Goes to Taxes?

At a typical Massachusetts salary of $99,900, a single filer loses 25.8% to taxes and keeps $74,115. The percentage is not one number — it is three, and they behave differently.

Two of those lines are flat nationwide: Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. The federal line is progressive, so its effective rate (13.2%) is well below the bracket you are in. The state line is what makes Massachusetts different from its neighbours.

What $99,900 means in Massachusetts

That gross is the Massachusetts median household income ($99,858, 2023), rounded to the nearest hundred. It is 129% of the national median household income of $77,719.

After every deduction you keep $74,115 a year — $6,176 a month, $2,851 per biweekly check. Rent at the common 30%-of-gross rule would take $2,498 a month, leaving $3,679.

At this level Massachusetts takes more than a quarter of gross pay. A $5,000 raise would add about $3,268 to take-home, because the marginal rate applies only to the extra.

How to lower the percentage

Pre-tax deductions come off before federal income tax: a 401(k), a health premium or an HSA. None of them reduce FICA, so the saving is real but partial. Putting 5% of $99,900 into a 401(k) removes $4,995 from the federal taxable base, roughly $1,099 of federal tax at this income. Filing jointly, a dependent-care account and the earned income credit change the result too.
