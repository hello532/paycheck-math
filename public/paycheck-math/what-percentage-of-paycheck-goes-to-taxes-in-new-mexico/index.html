What Percentage of a New Mexico Paycheck Goes to Taxes?

At a typical New Mexico salary of $62,300, a single filer loses 19.0% to taxes and keeps $50,476. The percentage is not one number — it is three, and they behave differently.

Two of those lines are flat nationwide: Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. The federal line is progressive, so its effective rate (8.5%) is well below the bracket you are in. The state line is what makes New Mexico different from its neighbours.

What $62,300 means in New Mexico

That gross is the New Mexico median household income ($62,268, 2023), rounded to the nearest hundred. It is 80% of the national median household income of $77,719.

After every deduction you keep $50,476 a year — $4,206 a month, $1,941 per biweekly check. Rent at the common 30%-of-gross rule would take $1,558 a month, leaving $2,649.

At this level New Mexico takes a little under a quarter of gross pay. A $5,000 raise would add about $3,703 to take-home, because the marginal rate applies only to the extra.

How to lower the percentage

Pre-tax deductions come off before federal income tax: a 401(k), a health premium or an HSA. None of them reduce FICA, so the saving is real but partial. Putting 5% of $62,300 into a 401(k) removes $3,115 from the federal taxable base, roughly $685 of federal tax at this income. Filing jointly, a dependent-care account and the earned income credit change the result too.
