What Percentage of a Utah Paycheck Goes to Taxes?

At a typical Utah salary of $93,400, a single filer loses 24.6% to taxes and keeps $70,381. The percentage is not one number — it is three, and they behave differently.

Two of those lines are flat nationwide: Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. The federal line is progressive, so its effective rate (12.5%) is well below the bracket you are in. The state line is what makes Utah different from its neighbours.

What $93,400 means in Utah

That gross is the Utah median household income ($93,421, 2023), rounded to the nearest hundred. It is 120% of the national median household income of $77,719.

After every deduction you keep $70,381 a year — $5,865 a month, $2,707 per biweekly check. Rent at the common 30%-of-gross rule would take $2,335 a month, leaving $3,530.

At this level Utah takes a little under a quarter of gross pay. A $5,000 raise would add about $3,295 to take-home, because the marginal rate applies only to the extra.

How to lower the percentage

Pre-tax deductions come off before federal income tax: a 401(k), a health premium or an HSA. None of them reduce FICA, so the saving is real but partial. Putting 5% of $93,400 into a 401(k) removes $4,670 from the federal taxable base, roughly $1,027 of federal tax at this income. Filing jointly, a dependent-care account and the earned income credit change the result too.
