What Percentage of a Minnesota Paycheck Goes to Taxes?

At a typical Minnesota salary of $85,100, a single filer loses 24.3% to taxes and keeps $64,434. The percentage is not one number — it is three, and they behave differently.

Two of those lines are flat nationwide: Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%. The federal line is progressive, so its effective rate (11.6%) is well below the bracket you are in. The state line is what makes Minnesota different from its neighbours.

What $85,100 means in Minnesota

That gross is the Minnesota median household income ($85,086, 2023), rounded to the nearest hundred. It is 109% of the national median household income of $77,719.

After every deduction you keep $64,434 a year — $5,370 a month, $2,478 per biweekly check. Rent at the common 30%-of-gross rule would take $2,128 a month, leaving $3,242.

At this level Minnesota takes a little under a quarter of gross pay. A $5,000 raise would add about $3,178 to take-home, because the marginal rate applies only to the extra.

How to lower the percentage

Pre-tax deductions come off before federal income tax: a 401(k), a health premium or an HSA. None of them reduce FICA, so the saving is real but partial. Putting 5% of $85,100 into a 401(k) removes $4,255 from the federal taxable base, roughly $936 of federal tax at this income. Filing jointly, a dependent-care account and the earned income credit change the result too.
